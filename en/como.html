How Nexus Terminal works

What you see on the site, explained without jargon: how a signal is detected, where a forecast comes from and how we measure whether it is right.

The vocabulary in one minute

Open, close and gap
The open is the first price of the day and the close the last. The gap is the difference between yesterday's close and today's open.
Bullish and bearish
An asset is bullish if it generally rises over time and bearish if it falls. If it does neither, it is in a range.
LONG and SHORT
LONG is betting the price rises (buying). SHORT is betting it falls (short selling), with different risks and requirements that not every broker allows.
Stop
An exit price set in advance to limit the loss. We suggest one based on volatility, but the decision is yours.

How a signal is detected

Trend

20- and 50-session averages

If the 20-session average is above the 50, the price has been rising (bullish); if below, falling. The strength is the distance between them.

RSI

Is it going too fast?

From 0 to 100, it measures the speed of the last 14 sessions. Above 70 is overbought (it may pull back); below 30, oversold (it may bounce).

ATR and extremes

Volatility and ceilings

The ATR is the average daily move and is used to suggest a stop. We also look at the high and low of the last 20 sessions.

LONG: uptrend, RSI below 68 and price within 3% of its 20-session high. SHORT: downtrend, RSI above 32 and price within 3% of its 20-session low. The same fixed rules for every asset, which is why we can honestly check afterwards how many times they worked.

Where a forecast comes from

1

Similar situations

For each asset we search the history for days with the same trend, RSI and volatility and look at what happened next. That gives a probability and a range of movement, corrected with how often the method was actually right in the past.

2

The chain of markets

Tokyo opens before Europe and New York before Tokyo the next day, so what one market does influences the next. In the history, the open of Madrid, London, Germany and France followed Tokyo's direction between 63% and 67% of days. The edge is in how the market opens: from open to close the hit rate is almost that of chance.

3

Engine 2

It mixes the chain, 5-day momentum, volatility and the calendar into a single probability. It is trained only on what came before each date and tested on days it had not seen. A forecast is only published if its measured hit rate clearly beats "always saying the same thing".

How to read reliability

"At this confidence (70% or more) it hit 79% of 211 days (safe floor 73%)" means that, in the past, when the engine was this sure, it got the direction right on 79 out of every 100 days it had not seen.

The safe floor is the statistical minimum: with few days a high hit rate can be luck, and that figure discounts it. Always compare with "always saying the same thing": if a market rises 58% of days, guessing "up" is already right 58% of the time.

Data and timing

  • The Scanner updates every night with the previous close; forecasts every morning (around 8:30 Spanish time), together with a single Telegram message.
  • US, crypto and forex come from Polygon.io (Massive). Madrid, London, Germany, France and Tokyo come from Yahoo Finance, an unofficial source with no guarantee of accuracy.
  • If the latest close of a market the engine needs is missing, that forecast is not published; and sessions that have already closed are not sent.

Frequently asked questions

Is the data live?

No, it is from the latest close. It is an analysis and preparation tool, not a trading terminal.

Does it guarantee results?

No. We measure how many times it was right in the past and show it as it is, misses included. It is not financial advice and every investment carries a risk of loss.

Why is there sometimes no forecast for a market?

Because some recent data is missing, the session has already closed or the measured reliability does not clearly beat chance. We prefer not to publish rather than publish something weak.

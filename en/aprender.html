Learn trading

A simple glossary of the words you will see in the Scanner and the forecasts. Click any term in the Scanner and you will land straight on its definition.

The basics

Stock (share)

A small piece of a company. If you buy shares you own a slice of it, and you gain or lose as its price goes up or down.

The basics

Open and close

The open is the first price of the day and the close is the last one. The Scanner works with the previous session's close.

The basics

Gap

The difference between yesterday's close and today's open. If it opens far above or below, a gap is left on the chart.

The basics

Session

One trading day of a market, from open to close. Each exchange has its own hours.

The basics

Volume

How many shares (or coins) were traded in a period. High volume usually confirms a move.

The basics

Liquidity

How easy it is to buy or sell without moving the price. Heavily traded assets are liquid; thinly traded ones are harder to sell.

The basics

Market capitalization

What a company is worth on the market: share price times number of shares. In our heat map we use money traded, which is different.

The basics

Index

A basket of securities that summarises a market, such as the S&P 500 or the Nasdaq 100. It moves with the group as a whole.

The basics

ETF

A fund that trades like a stock and tracks an index. In the top bar we follow the indices with ETFs such as SPY or QQQ.

The basics

Forex

The currency market: you buy one currency and sell another, for example EUR/USD. It is open 24 hours, Monday to Friday.

The basics

Crypto

Digital currencies such as Bitcoin or Ethereum. They trade every day, at any hour, and usually move more than stocks.

The basics

Bullish and bearish

Bullish: the price rises over time. Bearish: it falls. If it does neither, it is range-bound.

The basics

LONG and SHORT

LONG is betting the price will rise (buying). SHORT is betting it will fall (short selling); it carries more risks and not every broker allows it.

Indicators

Moving average (SMA)

The average price of the last N sessions. The 20 shows the short term and the 50 the medium term.

Indicators

Trend

Where the price is heading overall. Here it is bullish if the 20-session average is above the 50, and bearish if below.

Indicators

RSI

An indicator from 0 to 100 that measures the speed of the last 14 sessions. Above 70 usually signals overbought; below 30, oversold.

Indicators

Overbought and oversold

Overbought: it rose so fast it may pull back. Oversold: it fell so far it may bounce. It is not an order to act.

Indicators

ATR

The average daily move of the price over 14 sessions. It measures real volatility and helps set a sensible stop.

Indicators

Volatility

How much a price moves. More volatility means more opportunity but also more risk of quick losses.

Indicators

20-session high and low

The highest and lowest price of the last 20 sessions. A stock near the high is strong; near the low, weak.

Indicators

Support and resistance

Support: a level where the price tends to stop falling. Resistance: a level where it tends to stop rising.

Indicators

Market breadth

How many stocks rise versus how many fall. If an index rises but most stocks fall, the rise is fragile.

Risk and money

Stop (stop loss)

An exit price set in advance to cut a loss. We suggest one based on ATR, but the decision is yours.

Risk and money

Risk/reward ratio

How much you can gain for each unit you risk. A ratio of 2 means winning 2 if it works for each 1 lost if it fails.

Risk and money

Position size

How many units you buy. It is calculated so that, if the stop is hit, you lose only the percentage of capital you chose to risk.

Risk and money

Drawdown

The drop from the previous peak of your account or an asset. A 50 % drawdown needs a 100 % gain to recover.

Risk and money

Leverage

Trading with more money than you have, lent by the broker. It multiplies gains and losses: you can lose more than you put in.

Risk and money

Diversification

Spreading money across several assets so a single failure does not ruin everything.

Trading

Spread and commission

The spread is the gap between the buy and sell price; the commission is what the broker charges. Both reduce your profit.

Trading

Market and limit order

Market: executes now at the available price. Limit: executes only if the price reaches the level you set.

Trading

Position

What you currently have open: a purchase (long) or a short sale.

Trading

Broker

The company through which you buy and sell. Nexus Terminal is not a broker and does not execute trades.

In Nexus Terminal

Forecast

A statistical probability that the price will rise or fall in the next session, calculated from similar past situations. It is not a promise.

In Nexus Terminal

Engine 2

The model that blends the exchange chain, 5-day momentum, volatility and the calendar into a single probability. It is tested day by day without looking at the future.

In Nexus Terminal

Safe minimum

The statistical floor of the hit rate: with few days a high hit rate can be luck, and this figure discounts it.

In Nexus Terminal

Always saying the same

The benchmark we compare against: guessing without thinking by always saying ‘up’ (or ‘down’). A forecast is only worth something if it beats it.

In Nexus Terminal

Exchange chain

Tokyo opens before Europe, and New York after. What one exchange does influences the next.

In Nexus Terminal

Demonstrated edge

When the measured hit rate beats chance with statistical confidence. If it is not demonstrated we say so and do not list individual stocks.
